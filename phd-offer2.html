PhD Thesis Offer

Shared Agentivity: Modeling Collective Action and Decision-Making in Artificial Agent Assemblies

Project Description

This PhD project explores the concept of shared agentivity in artificial agent assemblies - the capacity for multiple AI agents to act as a cohesive whole with emergent properties beyond the capabilities of individual agents. The research will investigate theoretical frameworks and practical implementations for creating AI systems where collective agency emerges from interactions between autonomous agents.

Key research areas include:

  • Developing formal models of shared agency and collective intentionality in AI systems
  • Creating computational architectures that support emergent goal-directed behavior across agent assemblies
  • Investigating the relationship between individual agent autonomy and collective decision-making
  • Exploring ethical implications of artificial collective agency and responsibility attribution
  • Building experimental platforms to demonstrate and evaluate shared agentivity in practice

This interdisciplinary project will contribute to fundamental questions about the nature of agency in artificial systems while advancing practical applications in collaborative AI.

Requirements for PhD Candidate

  • Master's degree in Computer Science, Artificial Intelligence, Philosophy of AI, or related field
  • Strong programming skills and experience with multi-agent system implementations
  • Knowledge of contemporary AI architectures and large language models
  • Interest in philosophical questions related to agency, intentionality, and collective action
  • Ability to design and conduct rigorous empirical evaluations of agent systems
  • Excellent analytical and critical thinking skills
  • Strong written and verbal communication skills in English
  • Experience with formal modeling of agent behavior (desirable)
  • Background in cognitive science or philosophy of action (desirable)
